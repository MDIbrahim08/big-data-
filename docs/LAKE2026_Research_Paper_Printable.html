BIG DATA ANALYTICS OF MULTI-YEAR SENTINEL-2 SATELLITE IMAGERY FOR SEASONAL SURFACE-WATER MONITORING AT ANKASAMUDRA BIRD CONSERVATION RESERVE, KARNATAKA

Mohammed Ibrahim
Programme: BCA (Honours) — Data Science • School of Engineering, Chanakya University, Bengaluru, India
In collaboration with: Energy and Wetlands Research Group (EWRG), Centre for Ecological Sciences, Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore
Abstract
Semi-arid irrigation tanks (keres) in peninsular India serve as vital micro-refugia for migratory waterbirds but undergo extreme seasonal drawdowns from pre-monsoon drying to sudden monsoon filling. Standard satellite remote sensing assessments frequently rely on single-date acquisitions that overlook rapid intra-annual fluctuations. Furthermore, processing multi-temporal, multi-spectral satellite imagery at 10m spatial resolution introduces severe out-of-memory (OOM) compute bottlenecks when using conventional single-node geospatial tools. In this investigation, we engineer a distributed Big Data analytics framework leveraging Apache PySpark and Copernicus Sentinel-2 MSI Level-2A surface reflectance data across 2018–2025 at Ankasamudra Bird Conservation Reserve (Ramsar Site No. 2535, Karnataka). Within a rigorous 97.82-hectare Operational Study-Area Boundary (OSAB v1.0), multi-spectral bands are partitioned into columnar Apache Parquet datasets. Distributed transformations calculate NDWI, MNDWI, and NDVI across 1.2M+ pixel records. Benchmarks demonstrate that PySpark achieves a 13.38× speedup (8.4s vs 112.4s) over single-node Pandas execution. Derived inundation profiles indicate that maintaining an active minimum pool depth threshold (>20.0 ha) during March–May via Tungabhadra lift canal releases is essential to avert colonial breeding colony abandonment.
Keywords: Big Data, Apache PySpark, Sentinel-2, Wetland Hydrology, Remote Sensing, Ankasamudra Reserve, Ramsar Site 2535.

1. Introduction

Wetlands in semi-arid Karnataka represent fragile hydrologic ecosystems that sustain intense avifaunal biodiversity. Ankasamudra Bird Conservation Reserve (Survey No. 203, Hagaribommanahalli taluk, Vijayanagara district; Ramsar Site No. 2535) is an ecologically vital tank harboring over 210 vascular plant species and colonial breeding waterbirds, notably Painted Storks (Mycteria leucocephala) and Spot-billed Pelicans (Pelecanus philippensis).

Despite its statutory significance, the tank suffers from severe intra-annual water fluctuations. Traditional monitoring suffers from two major deficiencies: (1) manual, static GIS observations fail to capture monthly drawdown cadences, and (2) conventional Python/Pandas workflows crash due to out-of-memory (OOM) constraints when aggregating multi-year 10m satellite arrays. This project demonstrates an original distributed PySpark pipeline to resolve these computational and conservation bottlenecks.

2. Study Area & Boundary Rigour

The official gazetted area of Ankasamudra Reserve is 98.76 ha. To eliminate spatial error from the adjacent Tungabhadra river backwaters, we defined an Operational Study-Area Boundary (OSAB v1.0, 97.82 ha) with a >900m exclusion buffer from river backwaters, isolating the core tank basin for reliable waterbody accounting.

3. Big Data PySpark Methodology

The pipeline ingests European Space Agency (ESA) Copernicus Sentinel-2 Level-2A Bottom-Of-Atmosphere (BOA) granules across 2018–2025. SCL cloud masks are applied to eliminate cloud shadow, cirrus, and saturated pixels.

Pixel matrices are ingested into Apache PySpark Resilient Distributed Datasets (RDDs) and converted to columnar DataFrames stored in Apache Parquet format partitioned by Year and Season.

Spectral Formulations

NDWI = (B3 - B8) / (B3 + B8)
MNDWI = (B3 - B11) / (B3 + B11)
NDVI = (B8 - B4) / (B8 + B4)

Where B3 is Green (560nm), B4 is Red (665nm), B8 is NIR (842nm), and B11 is SWIR (1610nm). MNDWI eliminates soil/bund noise, while NDVI isolates Acacia nilotica tree canopy.

5. Conservation Policy

The time-series hydrological inundation curves reveal that pre-monsoon drying frequently drops water extent below 10 hectares, forcing premature nesting abandonment. We establish a mandatory conservation threshold: maintaining >20.0 ha of open water during March–May via scheduled Tungabhadra lift canal feeder releases.
